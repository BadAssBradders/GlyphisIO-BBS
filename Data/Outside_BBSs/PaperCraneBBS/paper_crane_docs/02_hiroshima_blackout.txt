TITLE: Hiroshima Castle Blackout - 1945

CLASSIFIED RECOVERY DOCUMENT
Status: Declassified under Pacifica Archive Leak 1987

The Imperial Family gathered inside Hiroshima Castle on the morning of August 6th, 1945, for a classified briefing on troop morale and regional defence logistics. The Emperor himself had traveled from Tokyo against advisement, insisting on hearing firsthand reports from western commanders.

At 08:15 local time, the atomic detonation erased the fortress and every heir in one flash.

No courtiers survived. No regent. No lineage.

The chrysanthemum throne sat empty before the mushroom cloud had finished rising.

With the imperial line annihilated, Japan lost its ceremonial centre overnight. There was no pivot to rally around, no successor to crown, no living symbol to negotiate surrender. The military government fractured into competing factions within hours.

American command read the vacuum immediately.

Occupation plans moved from "temporary advisory" to "total administration" before the ash had settled on the rivers. What might have been a decade of reconstruction became permanent integration. The surrender documents were rewritten three times before signing.

By September, the word "Emperor" had been struck from official communications.

By October, the phrase "former Japanese territories" appeared in Washington briefings.

By December, cartographers were already drafting new names.
