TITLE: Petition of the Silent Shrine - 1947

ADMINISTRATIVE RECORD: PETITION 1947-RC-0894
Filed by: Caretakers of Yamabiko Shrine, Nagano Prefecture
Re: Occupation Edict 48-C (Religious Site Decommissioning)

TO THE PROVISIONAL AUTHORITY OF THE PACIFICA ISLES:

We, the hereditary caretakers of Yamabiko Shrine, respectfully submit this petition against demolition order 48-C, scheduled for execution on March 15th, 1947.

ARGUMENTS FOR PRESERVATION:

1. CENSUS FUNCTION: This shrine has served as the rural census centre for seventeen surrounding villages since 1603. Birth records, death records, marriage certificates, and land deeds are stored in the eastern archive building. Removal will erase legal documentation for approximately 4,200 families. No alternative record system has been proposed.

2. AGRICULTURAL INFRASTRUCTURE: Local farmers rely on shrine-maintained wells and seed vaults predating the Meiji era. The seed collection contains forty-seven rice varieties adapted to high-altitude cultivation. Loss of these seeds will reduce regional crop diversity by an estimated sixty percent.

3. EMERGENCY SHELTER: Ceremonial grounds have doubled as typhoon shelters for three centuries. Engineering surveys confirm the main hall can shelter 800 persons. Projected storm casualties will increase by 12-15% annually without this facility.

4. HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE: The shrine contains the last known copies of the Yamabiko Chronicles, a record of local governance from 1185-1868. These documents exist nowhere else.

WE DO NOT ASK FOR WORSHIP.
WE ASK ONLY FOR CONTINUITY.

---

OUTCOME [Stamped March 3rd, 1947]:

PETITION DENIED.

Grounds bulldozed on schedule. Three stone tablets removed by occupation personnel - current location unknown, presumed private collection. The main archive was burned rather than relocated.

Caretaker Takeda Yoichi was detained for "obstruction" after attempting to remove seed containers. Released after forty days. Died of pneumonia the following winter.

The village elders recorded their families on rice paper the night before demolition. The papers were sealed in clay jars and buried beneath the western tree line.

Some say they are still there.
