TITLE: The Naming

PERSONAL TESTIMONY - ANONYMOUS SUBMISSION
Received by Paper Crane Network, March 1989
Sender: "FREQUENCY_GHOST" (unverified)

---

I found out on my sixteenth birthday.

Not as a gift. As an accident.

Mom was cleaning out the closet in her bedroom, the one she never let us touch. I was looking for my birth certificate because I needed it for my driver's permit. She said it was in the brown folder. She didn't say which brown folder.

There were two.

The first one had my birth certificate. KEVIN JAMES MOORE. Born March 3rd, 1973, Pacifica General Hospital, Neo-Tokyo District. Father: ROBERT ALLEN MOORE. Mother: HELEN GRACE MOORE. Everything I already knew.

The second folder was older. The paper was different - thinner, softer, slightly yellow at the edges. I almost didn't open it because it looked like tax stuff.

It wasn't tax stuff.

It was a birth certificate. Same date. Same hospital. Same parents.

Different name.

KENJI TAKAHIRO YAMAMOTO.

Father: TAKAHIRO YAMAMOTO.
Mother: HARUKA YAMAMOTO.

I sat on the floor of that closet for twenty minutes. I kept reading it over and over like the words would change if I looked hard enough.

They didn't change.

---

Mom found me there. She didn't yell. She just sat down next to me and didn't say anything for a long time.

Then she told me.

Grandpa was YAMAMOTO ICHIRO. He ran a printing shop in what they used to call Osaka before they renamed it Pacific West. When the Linguistic Normalisation Directive came through, he refused to change the sign. Said the name had been above that door for four generations and he'd burn the building down before he painted over it.

They didn't let him burn it down. They just took it.

Grandma was pregnant with Mom when it happened. They had three months to pick new names or lose their residency cards.

Grandpa picked MOORE because it was on a billboard across the street. MOORE INSURANCE. He said if he had to become someone else, he'd pick the first name he saw so he'd never forget it was arbitrary.

Mom was born HELEN. She never knew she was supposed to be HARUKA until she was older than I am now.

---

I asked her why she never told me.

She said: "What would you have done with it?"

I didn't have an answer.

She said: "You can't speak it. You can't write it. You can't put it on a form. It's just a sound now. A shape in your mouth that doesn't connect to anything. I didn't want you to carry that. I wanted you to be whole in the world you actually live in."

I told her that wasn't her choice to make.

She said: "I know."

---

I've been practicing. Late at night, when everyone's asleep.

Kenji.

It feels strange. Like wearing someone else's clothes. Like walking into a room and not recognising the furniture but knowing it's supposed to be yours.

I don't know if I'll ever feel like Kenji. I've been Kevin for sixteen years. That's who my friends know. That's who my teachers call. That's the name on my basketball jersey and my library card and every piece of paper that says I exist.

But I say it anyway. Every night before I sleep.

Kenji.

Because someone should.
